## Paper 3B venue comparison (live sources, 2026-11-16)

**Recommendation:** keep Machine Learning: Science and Technology (MLST) as the base target, with Journal of Computational Physics (JCP) second. Aim higher only if the confirmatory result has the pattern described under "When to aim at the top tier". There is one new strategic point. The 3B README says **"No confirmatory data exist yet."** So a Registered Report (pre-results review) is still possible. Among the journals in this comparison, only Nature Communications offers that format.

### Venue table

**Nature Computational Science**
- **Scope:** "development and use of computational techniques and mathematical models… methodologies that use computing capabilities in novel ways to find new insights".
- **Article type:** Article or Analysis. Main text up to 3,500 words excluding Methods, 6 display items, abstract up to 150 words. Analysis is defined as "new data obtained in a comparative analysis that leads to novel and arresting conclusions of importance to a broad audience".
- **Fee:** journal is hybrid. The subscription route has no fee.
- **Selectivity:** the Nature Portfolio page says papers judged "of insufficient general interest… are rejected promptly without external review". The bar is "an advance in understanding likely to influence thinking in the field".
- **Timing:** median 13 days to first decision, 193 days to acceptance.
- **Metrics:** JIF 20.3 (2025), 5-year JIF 21.9.
- **AI policy:** Springer Nature risk framework. "Green" (permitted) uses include "structuring or clarifying reviewer comments". "Red" (not permitted) includes fabricated citations and undisclosed core reasoning.
- **Null results:** no statement found.

**Nature Machine Intelligence**
- **Scope:** ML/AI and "the significant impact that these fields have on other scientific disciplines".
- **Article type:** same Article and Analysis limits. Reusability Reports exist but test published code, so they do not fit.
- **Fee:** hybrid; the subscription route has no fee.
- **Selectivity:** same desk-rejection rule as above.
- **Timing:** median 10 days to first decision, 231 days to acceptance.
- **Metrics:** JIF 29.8, 5-year JIF 36.0.
- **AI policy:** Springer Nature framework.
- **Null results:** no policy statement found. However, the playbook's must-cite McGreivy and Hakim paper on weak baselines has the DOI prefix 10.1038/s42256, which is this journal.

**PNAS**
- Blocked. University library pages found by search (not the PNAS page) give $6,075 for immediate open access and about $3,025 for delayed open access, which suggests **there is no route without a fee**. This is unverified.

**PNAS Nexus**
- Blocked. Fully open access and not on the OUP funded list, so there is no fee-free route unless a waiver is granted.

**Science Advances**
- **Scope:** "impactful research… in any area of science".
- **Article type:** Research Article up to 15,000 words, 10 display items, 80 references.
- **Fee:** APC $5,450, no TÜBİTAK route. A full waiver applies only to Research4Life A/B countries; I did not check whether Turkey qualifies.
- **Selectivity:** described as "highly selective"; the editorial board decides what goes to external review.
- **Timing and metrics:** not found on the pages opened.
- **AI policy:** disclose in the cover letter and in Methods or Acknowledgements; AI-authored sources may not be cited.
- **Null results:** not found.

**Nature Communications**
- **Scope:** all sciences; "important advances of significance to specialists".
- **Article type:** Article, main text about 5,000 words, 10 display items. **Registered Reports are offered** "for hypothesis-driven quantitative research with primary research data", with in-principle acceptance after Stage 1.
- **Fee:** APC £5,490 / $7,350 / €6,150, not TÜBİTAK-covered. Waivers are "considered on a case-by-case basis… in cases of financial need" and must be requested at submission.
- **Timing:** median 9 days to first decision, 242 days to acceptance.
- **Metrics:** JIF 18.1, 5-year JIF 18.9.
- **AI policy:** Springer Nature framework.
- **Null results:** the Registered Report format makes outcome-neutral publication possible.

**Journal of Computational Physics**
- **Scope:** "computational aspects of physical problems… the frontier between predictive simulation and machine learning. When addressing problems previously covered by other approaches, a comparison should be provided… efficacy, robustness, computational complexity, as well as reproducibility should be addressed." This is a strong fit for 3B's comparison against a coarse consistent solver.
- **Article type:** no formal page limit.
- **Fee:** hybrid, so the subscription route has no fee. The open-access APC would be $4,240 (Elsevier price list dated 05-Oct-2026).
- **Timing:** blocked. The hub figures (7 days to first decision, 211 days to acceptance) were not re-verified.
- **Metrics:** IF 3.9, 5-year IF 4.7 (shop.elsevier.com).
- **AI policy:** Elsevier allows AI tools "with human oversight" and requires a disclosure statement at submission.
- **Null results:** not stated.

**Computer Methods in Applied Mechanics and Engineering (CMAME)**
- **Scope:** "physically-based machine-learning methods and digital-twin technologies"; fields include heat transfer and fluid mechanics. It publishes "significant developments of computational methods". A falsification study is not a method development.
- **Fee:** hybrid; the subscription route has no fee. The open-access APC would be $4,780.
- **Selectivity:** Paper 1 was desk-rejected here on 2026-10-17 (project memory).
- **Metrics:** IF 7.6, 5-year IF 8.3.
- **AI policy:** Elsevier.
- **Null results:** not stated.

**Transactions on Machine Learning Research (TMLR)**
- **Scope:** "accounts of applications of existing techniques that shed light on the strengths and weaknesses of the methods" and "reproducibility studies".
- **Article type:** any length.
- **Fee:** "no fees or payments to authors".
- **Review:** double-blind and open on OpenReview. The action editor can desk-reject within 1 week. Reviews are due within 2 weeks (longer if the paper exceeds 12 pages).
- **Null results:** the most explicitly welcoming venue here. Interest "should not be used as a reason to reject work that isn't considered 'significant'… novelty… is not a necessary criterion".
- **AI policy:** LLMs allowed "as general-purpose assistive tools".
- **Metrics:** none shown.
- **Catch:** double-blind review conflicts with the named Zenodo protocol and public repository, so these would need anonymising.

**Machine Learning: Science and Technology (MLST)**
- **Scope:** "Simulation of materials, chemical processes and biological systems", "New (physics inspired) learning algorithms", "Benchmark studies".
- **Fee:** gold open access, covered by TÜBİTAK.
- **Selectivity:** IOP runs pre-review checks for scope and quality.
- **Metrics:** blocked. The hub's IF 4.2 was not re-verified.
- **AI policy:** IOP forbids AI-generated or AI-modified reference lists, uploading reviewer reports to AI, and AI-generated responses to reviewers. The disclosure template says "reviewed, validated and critically assessed". Conflicts A and B in the playbook still stand.
- **Null results:** no statement found.

**Computers & Chemical Engineering**

- **Fee:** hybrid; the subscription route has no fee. The open-access APC would be $3,710.
- **Metrics:** IF 4.9, 5-year IF 4.5.
- **AI policy:** Elsevier.

**Chemical Engineering Science**
- **Scope:** "selectively publish outstanding research that has as its foundations the Science of Chemical Engineering". This is a weak fit for an ML falsification study.
- **Fee:** hybrid; the subscription route has no fee. The open-access APC would be $3,770.
- **Metrics:** IF 5.1.

**AIChE Journal**
- **Scope:** "significant research results in all areas of chemical engineering… highest standards in rigor, novelty, and innovation". It has a Process Systems Engineering section.
- **Article type:** Research Article under 35 pages double-spaced, including figures.
- **Fee:** hybrid; subscription route free, and open access is TÜBİTAK-funded.
- **Selectivity:** 26% acceptance rate; median 10 days to first decision.
- **Metrics:** CiteScore 7.9, JIF 4.4.
- **AI policy:** Wiley "welcome[s] the responsible use"; authors remain accountable.

### Ranking by expected value (prestige × realistic chance), as it stands
These are judgements, not measured probabilities.

1. **MLST.** The scope text names this kind of study, there is no fee, and the article form fits 8,300 words. The main risks are the IOP AI-policy conflicts and the unverified metrics.
2. **JCP.** The scope explicitly demands comparison with existing approaches and reproducibility, which 3B delivers through its solver baseline. The subscription route is free. It is the best choice if the author cannot accept IOP's AI rules.
3. **Nature Machine Intelligence (Analysis) or Nature Computational Science (Analysis or Article), conditional on the result.** Very high prestige and a free subscription route. A desk rejection costs only about 10–13 days (median first decision), so one attempt is cheap if the result qualifies. The chance is low for a mixed result on two simulated 1-D systems.
4. **Computers & Chemical Engineering.** "Comparisons of alternative methodologies" is in the scope, the chance of acceptance is high, and prestige is mid-level.
5. **CMAME.** Higher IF, but the scope is about method development and Paper 1 was desk-rejected there.
6. **TMLR.** The best fit for a null result and no fee, but no IF is shown and double-blind review clashes with the named protocol.
7. **AIChE Journal.** Funded, but it asks for "novelty and innovation", has a 26% acceptance rate, and overlaps with Paper 4.
8. **Chemical Engineering Science.**
9. **Nature Communications.** Its Registered Report is the only top-tier pre-results route, but the £5,490 APC is not covered and depends on a discretionary waiver.

Science Advances, PNAS and PNAS Nexus are excluded because they have no fee-free route (PNAS and PNAS Nexus could not be verified directly).

### When to aim at the top tier
Submit to NMI or NCS first only if the frozen decision list gives a decisive verdict that is the same in A-primary, the Al-fumarate replication and system B, with intervals clear of the bands. Either of these would qualify:
- **A clear positive:** the structural constraint gives a large long-horizon gain over the information-matched and projection-matched controls, which isolates "constraint" as the mechanism.
- **A clear negative:** the compensated coarse solver at equal cost beats every learned arm in both systems, with the cause measured. This has the same general "weak baselines" message as McGreivy and Hakim, published in NMI.

Mixed, INCONCLUSIVE or single-setting results go to MLST or JCP.

If the author wants a top-tier outcome-neutral route, Stage 1 has to be submitted before any confirmatory run. That costs months of Stage 1 review, depends on an APC waiver, and the format's wording ("primary research data") may not cover simulation studies. Ask the editors first.
